How this dataset is built

Every record's title and abstract is classified by an ensemble of LLMs (GLM, Claude Sonnet, DeepSeek) against the health-financing-function taxonomy — Revenue raising, Pooling, Purchasing, Benefits decisions, Public financial management, plus recurrent and capital-investment financing — with a consensus step across models. Records that no model places in any bucket, or that stay Unclear after consensus, are screened out before full extraction.

Records that pass the bucket screen go through full field extraction: study design, type of analysis, data type and source, unit of observation, geography, outcome domain, and (for Revenue-raising studies) sub-questions on external/donor and non-governmental financing mechanisms. A DOI-recovery and MeSH/OpenAlex topic harvest runs on the extracted set.

How to read the numbers

A study can cover more than one financing function or outcome area. It is counted once in each category it is tagged with. This means percentages in the Explorer can add up to more than 100%.

Multi-country studies are counted once in each country they cover. In country-level views, the same study can therefore appear under several countries. The “Studies in view” total still counts each study only once.

Country-level views use the geographic information extracted from each study and match it to a standard country reference list. A small number of places identified in the studies — including Taiwan, Israel, Kazakhstan and Cuba — are not included in that reference list. They therefore do not appear in the map, country table or country profiles, but the studies are still included in all other charts and totals.

“NA” values are not shown as a separate category. If a study could not be assigned a financing function or outcome area, it simply has no tag for that dimension rather than being placed in an artificial “unknown” category. “Other” and “Unclear” are retained as visible categories where they were assigned during classification.

Limitations

Classification is LLM-based with a cross-model consensus step, not independently human-verified. Geography is free text matched against a fixed country list, so genuine geographies outside that list are excluded from country-level figures (see above). DOI and MeSH/topic coverage are partial. Disease-burden and health-spending figures are country-level reference data, not something extracted from the studies — comparisons against them are ecological, not causal.

Key financing topics of interest

These categories describe the health-financing functions and illustrative topics considered relevant to the evidence map.

Revenue raising

Definition. The ways money is brought into the health system through public, external and private sources, including taxes, insurance contributions, out-of-pocket payments and development assistance.

Illustrative topics. Revenue levels, trends and sources; domestic resource mobilisation; fiscal space; taxation and health taxes; earmarking; financial gap analysis; development assistance, donor dependency, transitions, remittances and fungibility.

Pooling

Definition. The accumulation of prepaid funds that can be used to purchase goods and services on behalf of a population and redistribute financial risk.

Illustrative topics. Insurance and other pooling mechanisms; adverse selection; pool fragmentation; cross-programmatic efficiency; intergovernmental transfers; equalisation formulae; risk adjustment; and conditional or unconditional grants.

Purchasing and provider payment

Definition. The use of pooled funds to pay for health care on behalf of a population, including active and passive purchasing arrangements.

Illustrative topics. Budgets, capitation, salaries, fee-for-service, case-based payment, pay-for-performance, performance- or results-based financing, direct facility funding, contracting, supplier-induced demand, and costing or pricing analyses that substantively inform payment decisions.

Benefits decisions, coverage and rationing

Definition. Decisions about who is covered, what pooled revenues will pay for, and the restrictions or conditions governing access.

Illustrative topics. Benefit-package design; prioritisation methods such as MCDA and PBMA; user charges and exemptions; benefit maximums; informal payments; waiting lists, exclusions and referral requirements; and economic evaluations of alternative service-delivery approaches.

Public financial management

Definition. The rules and mechanisms governing the allocation, use and accountability of public funds, including health-budget formulation, execution, monitoring and transparency.

Illustrative topics. Budget setting and structure; programme-based budgeting; expenditure tracking; fund-flow bottlenecks; digital PFM reforms; provider autonomy; reporting, transparency, accountability and anti-corruption.

Capital investment: infrastructure

Definition. Policies and practices to acquire, upgrade or maintain physical facilities, medical equipment, technology and digital infrastructure across the health system.

Illustrative topics. Infrastructure financing systems; investment levels; medical-equipment and technology investment; and implications for recurrent costs.

Capital investment: other health-system inputs

Definition. Policies and practices to develop the health workforce and invest in the production of medicines, vaccines and other health products.

Illustrative topics. Workforce education, recruitment, retention and incentives, and policy mechanisms supporting industries that produce medicines, vaccines, medical goods and other health-system inputs.

Supply-chain financing

Definition. Policies and practices that finance health supply chains and support the optimal use of commodities, infrastructure, staffing, logistics, information and data systems.

Illustrative topics. Planning and demand forecasting; sourcing and procurement; contracting; warehousing, transport and inventory management; information systems; governance and regulation; commodity financing; and supply-chain monitoring.

Cross-cutting financing analysis

Illustrative topics. Health-financing assessments, policy analysis and political economy of financing choices and reforms, and studies examining alignment across financing functions.

Outcome-domain definitions

Seven definitions draw directly from, or use language from, the WHO Health System Performance Assessment framework. Economic development is defined specifically for this evidence map.

Other indicates an identifiable outcome that falls outside the eight predefined outcome domains.

Unclear indicates that the available title and abstract did not provide enough information to classify the outcome confidently.

Outcome domainDefinition and coding guidance
Equitable distribution of health-system resourcesThe extent to which the distribution of health care and its benefits among a population is fair. Health-service equity refers to the distribution of care quality and ensuring that it does not vary because of personal characteristics such as gender, ethnicity, geographic location or socioeconomic status. Code this outcome when a study assesses an outcome for an equity-relevant group relative to another demographic or socioeconomic group, or uses an explicit comparative measure of inequality or equity. Do not code it solely because the study covers an equity-relevant or disadvantaged group.
Efficiency in the use of resourcesThe relationship between a specific product (output) of the health system and the resources (inputs) used to create the product, distinguishing technical and allocative efficiency.
Quality of service provisionThe degree to which health services for individuals and populations increase the likelihood of desired health outcomes and are consistent with current medical knowledge.
Transparency and accountabilityTransparency is about the public availability of usable information, which ultimately allows scrutiny of public actors and their decisions. Accountability refers to how well the system is governed overall, as well as how well the governance of financing, service delivery and resource generation performs. At the heart of accountability is an accountability relationship: someone is accountable to someone else for something.
Improved level and distribution of healthHealth improvement refers to improvement in the health of the population, where health includes different parts of the life cycle, morbidity and premature mortality.
Responsiveness to citizensThe extent to which the service-user perspective and experience of health care is measured and valued as an outcome of service delivery, including outcomes related to the perspectives and social preferences of individuals, carers, families and communities as participants in, and beneficiaries of, trusted health systems organised around people's comprehensive needs rather than individual diseases.
Financial protectionSafeguarding people against the financial hardship associated with paying for health services.
Economic developmentThe process by which the economic wellbeing and quality of life of a nation, region, local community or individual are improved according to targeted goals and objectives. Examples of impacts that health financing may have on economic development include, but are not limited to, labour-force opportunities, job creation, job mobility, business growth, investment decisions or macroeconomic growth. Goals, objectives or outcomes related only to the bottom line or financial health of a health-care organisation or private-sector entity are not classified as economic development.

Source: WHO, Health System Performance Assessment: A framework for policy analysis ↗

Health financing functions covered

Empirical evidence on health systems financing functions and their links to outcomes. Specifically, our focus is on recurrent financing of health services (revenue raising, pooling, purchasing, decisions on benefits and their rationing, and public financial management); capital financing to generate health system resources (e.g., infrastructure, human resources for health, and the production of health products); and supply chain financing.

Outcome domains mapped

The map covers eight outcome domains: equitable distribution of health-system resources; efficiency in the use of resources; quality of service provision; transparency and accountability; improved level and distribution of health; responsiveness to citizens; financial protection; and economic development.

Apart from Economic development, definitions draw directly from, or use language from, the WHO Health System Performance Assessment framework.

Other is used for identifiable outcomes outside these domains; Unclear is used when the available record does not support confident classification.

About the data: This release analyses 36,495 records from the HSF extraction dated 29 September, 2026. Classification is LLM-assisted with rigorous human verification.
